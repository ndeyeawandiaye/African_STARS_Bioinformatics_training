Multiple sequence alignment

Module 2 — 45 min

Idea

A multiple sequence alignment puts homologous positions in the same column, so that each column can be compared between genomes. Phylogenetics and SNP counting both start from it.

  • Consensus genomes obtained by mapping are already on the reference coordinates, but genomes downloaded from public databases can have different lengths, insertions and deletions.
  • MAFFT is a fast, standard aligner for viral genomes.
  • Always look at the alignment (AliView, Jalview): never trust it blindly.
  • Columns full of N or gaps carry no information.

Step 1 — Put all the genomes in one file

We combine the lineage references downloaded from NCBI Virus and your consensus from the previous exercises. seqkit seq rewrites the file cleanly, and sed renames the header of the consensus.

mkdir -p results/phylo
seqkit seq data/ncbi/lineage_refs.clean.fasta > results/phylo/all.fasta
sed 's/^>.*/>SRR26396743/' results/consensus/SRR26396743.fa | seqkit seq >> results/phylo/all.fasta
grep -c '>' results/phylo/all.fasta
grep '>' results/phylo/all.fasta

Question: how many sequences are there, and which one is your sample?

TipAnswer

The number of references you downloaded plus one (your consensus, named SRR26396743).

Step 2 — Check the quality of each genome before aligning

Count the N in each sequence:

seqkit fx2tab -n -l -B N results/phylo/all.fasta | awk '{printf "%s\t%d bp\t%d N\t%.2f%%\n", $1, $2, $3, 100*$3/$2}'

Questions

  1. Which genome has the most N?
  2. Is your sample good enough to be placed on a tree? (remember the exercise Is this genome good enough?)
TipAnswer

Use your own values. A genome with a large percentage of N (the course threshold is 10 %) has a position in the tree that is less reliable, because missing data can make it look closer to some genomes than it really is. If a reference has many N, replace it in NCBI Virus with a better one.

Step 3 — Align with MAFFT

mafft --auto --preservecase --thread 2 results/phylo/all.fasta > results/phylo/aln.fasta

(--preservecase keeps our upper-case letters. For a few dozen genomes of 30 kb this takes seconds to minutes.)

seqkit stats results/phylo/aln.fasta

Question: how many sequences and how many alignment columns? Why is the number of columns slightly larger than the genome length?

TipAnswer

The number of sequences is the same as in all.fasta. The number of columns is at least the length of the longest genome: when one genome has an insertion or a different start or end, MAFFT adds gaps (-) in the others so that homologous positions stay in the same column.

Step 4 — Inspect the alignment

Open results/phylo/aln.fasta in AliView or Jalview (https://www.jalview.org).

Questions

  1. Are the ends of the genomes aligned in the same way for all the sequences?
  2. Do you see blocks of - or N? In which genomes?
  3. Are the differences between genomes spread over the genome or concentrated in some regions?
TipAnswer

Look at your own alignment. The ends of public genomes are often ragged (different start and stop, or poly-A tails), blocks of N come from regions that were not covered, and differences between lineages are concentrated in some genes (the spike gene carries many lineage-defining changes). Real surveillance pipelines trim the ragged ends and mask known problematic sites before building trees.

Step 5 — SNP distances

How many differences between your sample and each genome? snp-dists ignores N and gaps in each pair:

snp-dists -m results/phylo/aln.fasta | awk '$1=="SRR26396743" && $2!="SRR26396743"' | sort -k3,3n | column -t

Questions

  1. Which genomes are the closest to your sample, and how many SNPs apart?
  2. How many SNPs separate your sample from Wuhan-Hu-1 (NC_045512.2)?
TipAnswer

Read the values in your own table. The closest genomes have the smallest numbers; the distance to Wuhan-Hu-1 gives the amount of evolution since the beginning of the pandemic. Keep this table, we use it for the lineage assignment.
