Communicating genomic evidence

Module 4 — 45 min

A genome analysis is useful only if the person who has to decide (a ministry, a hospital, a field team) understands it and knows how far to trust it. This module is about turning results into clear and honest statements.

Principles

  1. Start with the question the decision maker asks, and answer it first.
  2. Combine genomic and epidemiological evidence (dates, places, contacts). Genomes alone rarely prove transmission.
  3. Say how sure you are, and why.
  4. Show the limits: sampling bias, low resolution, missing data, excluded genomes.
  5. Make it reproducible: tool versions, parameters, files.

Language of certainty

Evidence Wording
Identical or nearly identical genomes, compatible dates and places “genomic data are consistent with a common source”
Same lineage, no finer resolution “we cannot distinguish between local transmission and repeated introductions”
Different lineages “not consistent with a single chain of transmission”
Low coverage / many N “insufficient data to conclude”

Avoid: “proves”, “confirms”, “patient X infected patient Y”.

A good figure

  • one message per figure, and the title states the message (not just “Phylogenetic tree”);
  • colour = a variable the reader cares about (region, lineage), with a legend;
  • label the sample(s) of interest; the caption gives the number of genomes and the method;
  • readable in grayscale and for colour-blind readers.

Exercise 1 — Rewrite these statements (20 min)

Rewrite each sentence so that the data support it.

  1. “The Region C genomes prove the outbreak came from Region A.”
  2. “S03 is negative for the virus.”
  3. “The tree shows that S01 infected OB10.”
TipSuggested answers
  1. “The Region C genomes belong to lineages that descend from the lineage first seen in Region A, which is consistent with an introduction from Region A followed by local transmission. Genomic data alone cannot establish the direction; travel and contact data are needed.” (Why: a tree shows relatedness, not direction; “prove” is too strong.)
  2. “S03 gave insufficient data (mean depth 5x, 95 % of the genome undetermined, in a run whose negative control had viral reads). The result is inconclusive, not negative: the sample should be re-sequenced.” (Why: a failed sequencing is not a negative test.)
  3. “S01 and OB10 are closely related (2 SNPs apart) and both belong to the early Region A cluster. This is compatible with a close epidemiological link, but the genomes alone do not show who infected whom.” (Why: no direction; other chains can produce the same pattern.)

Exercise 2 — Write a key message (15 min)

Write two sentences that answer: “Where does S01 fit in the outbreak, and how sure are you?”

TipExample

“S01 (Region A, 2 March) belongs to lineage TV.2, the early lineage of the outbreak, and is 2 SNPs from three other early Region A genomes, which is consistent with it being part of the same cluster. The genome is complete (99.4 % covered at ≥ 10x), but with only a few SNPs of difference, the resolution does not allow us to identify direct transmission links.”
